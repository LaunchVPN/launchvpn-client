LaunchVPN Privacy Policy

Effective November 5, 2026 · Русский

This policy describes what data the LaunchVPN app for Android and iOS sends, to whom and why, how long it is kept, and how to turn it off or delete it. The app and the servers belong to the LaunchVPN service. Questions about your data go to support in Telegram.

In short

  • The app has no analytics, no ads and no advertising identifier.
  • The app does not record or send anywhere which sites you open or which addresses you look up.
  • To count the devices on a subscription, the app sends our panel a device identifier and the device model.
  • Crash reports leave without links, addresses or email. You can turn them off.
  • You can delete your account right in the app.

Subscription and devices

When the app loads or updates a subscription, it sends our server panel:

  • a device identifier (HWID). On Android it is the identifier the system gives the app, and it changes after a factory reset. On iOS it is a random identifier the app creates on first launch and keeps in the Keychain, so it survives a reinstall;
  • the operating system, its version and the device model;
  • the app version.

The panel uses the identifier to count how many devices use the subscription and to turn away those over the limit. The model and the system let you recognize the device in the Devices list. A device is kept until you remove it from that list in the app, in the account Cabinet or in the bot.

Like any server, the panel sees the IP address a request comes from.

Connection

While the VPN is on, your traffic goes through our servers. A server sees your IP address to set up the connection, and where the traffic goes to pass it on. The panel counts the traffic volume of a subscription to apply its limits.

Before connecting, the app checks that the servers answer: through each of them it requests a service page from Google or Cloudflare. These requests carry no personal data.

Account

An account is optional: a subscription link works without one. If you sign in, our server keeps:

  • your email and password. The password is stored only as a hash; we do not know the password itself;
  • if you signed in with Telegram or linked it: your Telegram ID, first name, last name and username from Telegram;
  • the language for emails with codes;
  • the identifier and platform (Android or iOS) of the devices you signed in from;
  • your subscription, its end date and the payments made in the account Cabinet or in the bot.

On sign-in and sign-up the server looks at the IP address to limit the number of attempts. The address is not stored. The app keeps its sign-in keys in the system's secure storage: the Keychain on iOS and the Keystore on Android.

Account data is kept until you delete the account.

Deleting your account

Open Account and tap Delete account. The subscription and the days left on it are lost, including those received in the bot if Telegram is linked. Deletion cannot be undone.

  • If there were no payments, we delete everything: the account, the user in the panel and the devices.
  • If there were payments, we delete access: the user in the panel, email sign-in and sign-in keys. The record of payments stays, together with the Telegram ID if one was linked. It is needed to account for the payments.

Crash reports

If the app crashes or runs into an error, it sends a report to Sentry, a crash reporting service. Reports are kept on Sentry's servers in Germany for 90 days.

A report holds the error type, the place in the code where it happened, the app version, the device model, the system version and a random installation identifier that shows how many devices hit the same crash.

Before sending, the app removes from the report links, including the subscription link, email addresses, IP addresses, server and site names, device identifiers and the device name. Sentry does not store the IP address a report comes from.

Sending is on by default, and the app says so on first launch. You can turn it off in Settings with the Crash reports switch. While it is off, the app sends nothing.

What stays on the device

  • Subscriptions and settings are stored only on the device.
  • In Apps mode on Android, the app reads the list of installed apps so you can choose which go through the VPN. The list never leaves the device.
  • Renewal reminders are local notifications. The app is not connected to any push notification service.
  • The app does not ask for location, contacts, camera or photos.

Other requests

  • The Android version downloaded from GitHub asks GitHub for the latest version number at launch, to offer an update. Versions from the App Store and Google Play leave updates to the store.
  • Signing in with Telegram opens Telegram's page in the browser. What it collects is described in Telegram's privacy policy.
  • The account Cabinet, payment and support open in the browser or in Telegram, outside the app.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, its correction or its deletion by writing to support. You can also delete the account yourself as described above.

Changes
